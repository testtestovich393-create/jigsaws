A jigsaw puzzle you solve together. One file, no server, no accounts.
Players connect directly, peer-to-peer, via invite codes.

Everything runs in your browser. The picture and the game live only on the players' devices and are shared directly between them.

How it works

Playing

Drag pieces with the mouse or finger. Wheel / pinch zooms. With a mouse, the table pans only while the middle mouse button is held; on touch screens drag with one finger or pinch. A piece glows in a player's colour while they hold it — only one player can hold a group at a time. Pieces snap to each other when the fit is close, and to the frame when they reach their place.

Your board is saved automatically in this browser — close the tab any time and press Continue last session on the next visit to pick up exactly where you left off (pieces, picture and view are all kept on this device).

Multiplayer without a server

InviteAny player presses Invite and sends the code to a friend; the friend sends a reply code back. That single exchange is the whole signup.
MeshRight after joining, players introduce each other to everyone else and the session becomes a full peer-to-peer mesh of WebRTC channels. There is no host and no relay.
Session lifeThe session exists as long as at least one player keeps the page open. Everyone can leave and come back with a fresh invite at any time; late joiners receive the full board state.
Live movesWhile you drag, piece positions stream to everyone ~30 times per second and are interpolated, so pieces glide on remote screens.
LocksGrabbing a group locks it instantly. Two players grabbing the same group at the same moment resolve deterministically (earliest logical clock, then player id) — the loser feels the piece pulled away.
PictureThe image travels peer-to-peer in hash-checked chunks — it never touches any server.

Network notes

Connections are direct browser-to-browser (WebRTC). Public STUN servers are used only to discover your address when players are behind different routers; on the same network the app works fully offline. Some very restrictive corporate NATs can still block direct links (no TURN relay is used by design).
